How Much Does a Level 2 EV Charger Cost in Vancouver?

A breakdown of typical installation pricing, what drives the cost up, permit requirements, and the rebates that can bring it back down.

By Denmaq August 21, 2026 8 min read

The honest answer to "how much does an EV charger cost" is: it depends almost entirely on what's already in your electrical panel, not on the charger itself.

Two homes on the same Vancouver street can get wildly different quotes for what looks like the same job — one because the panel has room for a new 240V circuit, the other because it doesn't. This guide breaks down what a standard Level 2 install actually costs in Metro Vancouver, the handful of factors that swing the price the most, the permit process, and the rebates that can knock a few hundred dollars off the final bill.

01

Typical Installation Costs

Scenario Typical Cost What's Involved
Best case $800–$1,800 200-amp panel with available breaker space, charger location within a few metres of the panel
Standard installation $1,000–$2,500 New 240V dedicated circuit, permit, and a mid-range Level 2 charger unit
Load management add-on +$800–$1,200 A device that shares capacity with existing loads instead of requiring a panel upgrade
Older panel needing an upgrade +$1,800–$3,500 (or $4,500–$7,500 for a full 100A-to-200A upgrade) Common in pre-1960s Vancouver homes still running 100-amp service
Detached garage or laneway home +$2,000–$5,000 Underground trenching to run power to a structure separate from the main panel

Across Metro Vancouver, a standard installation with no major complications generally lands in the $900–$3,500 range all-in, including the charger unit, labour, and permit. More involved jobs — panel upgrades, trenching, or both — can run $5,000–$8,000.

02

What Drives the Price Up

01 /

Panel capacity

This is the single biggest swing factor. A modern 200-amp panel with room for a new breaker keeps the job simple. An older or already-full panel means either a load management device or a full panel upgrade before the charger can go in at all.

02 /

Distance from panel to charger

Every extra metre of conduit and wire adds labour and material cost. A charger mounted right next to the panel is the cheapest possible layout; a detached garage or laneway home usually means trenching.

03 /

Charger unit and features

Basic plug-in units start lower; smart, WiFi-enabled chargers with load-sharing and scheduling features cost more. The unit itself typically runs $400–$1,200 depending on brand and features.

04 /

Home age and wiring

Pre-1960s Vancouver homes often still run 100-amp service, which frequently can't support an EV circuit alongside existing loads without an upgrade — a cost worth confirming before you commit to a charger purchase.

Pro tip: Ask an electrician for a panel capacity assessment before you buy a charger. A $150 assessment can save you from buying a unit that needs a $4,000 panel upgrade to actually run.

03

Permits and Rebates

✓

Electrical permit required

A Level 2 install needs a permit from Technical Safety BC, since it involves a new dedicated 240V circuit. The permit fee typically runs $100–$200 and is usually handled by the electrician.

✓

BC Hydro Level 2 rebate

Eligible single-family homeowners can get up to $350 toward the purchase and installation of a qualifying, ENERGY STAR-certified Level 2 charger installed by a licensed electrician.

✓

Load management rebate

A separate rebate is available for installing an EV power management device — the same device that can let you avoid a full panel upgrade in the first place.

✓

Strata approval, if applicable

BC's Strata Property Act was amended to make it easier for strata owners to install chargers — a strata council cannot unreasonably deny a resident's application, though both strata and electrical permits are still required.

04

Red Flags in a Quote

✕

No mention of a permit

An electrician who doesn't bring up a Technical Safety BC permit isn't planning to pull one — a problem for insurance and resale if the work is never inspected.

✕

No panel assessment before quoting

A firm price given without checking your panel's available capacity is a guess, not a quote — and it's the most common reason installs run over budget.

✕

A quote well under the standard range

Pricing significantly below $800–$1,000 for a standard job often means a lower-tier charger unit, an unlicensed installer, or work that skips the permit process entirely.

✕

No rebate guidance

A licensed electrician doing this work regularly should be able to tell you which BC Hydro rebates apply to your situation without you having to ask first.

05

The Bottom Line

Most Vancouver homeowners with a modern panel and a reasonably close garage should expect to pay somewhere between $900 and $2,500 for a complete Level 2 install, before any rebate. Older homes and detached garages push that number higher, sometimes well past $5,000 once panel upgrades or trenching enter the picture.

Get a panel assessment before you buy a charger, confirm the permit is included in any quote, and check what you qualify for from BC Hydro before writing the final check. Know your panel before you know your price.
